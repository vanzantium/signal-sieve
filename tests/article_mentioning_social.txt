Last week, Google, Microsoft, Anthropic, and OpenAI all announced plans to support a new mechanism that lets web publishers express their desire to opt out of having their content used to train AI models. The proposal attracted considerable attention across the tech press and on social media. The mechanism is not an official standard — but adoption by the four largest search and AI providers might make it a de facto one. The proposal announced on October 30, 2026 is documented in what is colloquially known as an individual draft — a submission made by an author, which has no formal standing of any kind in the standards process. Anyone can submit an individual draft, and over 1,000 are submitted each year. The Code commits signatories to follow standardized crawler instructions only, and the proposal has no official status. Reports from the working group indicate that two other drafts are further along: a vocabulary draft and an attachment draft, both adopted by the working group. The analysis concludes that adoption, not formal status, will decide whether the mechanism matters.
