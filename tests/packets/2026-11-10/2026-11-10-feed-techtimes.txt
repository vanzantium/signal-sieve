SECONDARY — TechTimes, "Anthropic Cuts Claude Fable 5.2 Price 20%, Says Safeguards Block 55% Fewer Requests"
URL: https://www.techtimes.com/articles/330367/20261110/anthropic-cuts-claude-fable-52-price-20-says-safeguards-block-55-fewer-requests.htm
Published: 2026-11-10.

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 on Monday, Nov. 9, cutting Fable's API price 20% to $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, while its safeguards blocked about 55% fewer requests than Fable 5.1's in early testing. Developers get a cheaper, faster model today, but only vetted organizations can use Mythos 5.2, the same model with fewer restrictions.

A team of about 1,000 Fable 5.2 agents formalized Fermat's Last Theorem in the Lean proof assistant in 30 hours, and the outside evaluator METR estimated on Nov. 5 that Anthropic is improving its models on well-specified tasks at about twice its earlier pace.

Anthropic says both models generate output up to 30% faster than the 5.1 versions. Fable 5.2 is available across Anthropic's products, Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure. Mythos 5.2 stays limited to organizations in Anthropic's verification programs.

Anthropic describes Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 as the same underlying model. Fable carries cybersecurity and biology safeguards, while Mythos has fewer restrictions and goes to vetted organizations in dual-use fields. The split repeats a pattern: Anthropic disclosed Claude Mythos Preview on April 7, limited to Project Glasswing partners; Fable 5 and Mythos 5 followed on June 9. A U.S. export-control directive then forced Anthropic to disable both models for all customers on June 12, and access returned on July 1. Tiered access has already survived one government intervention.

On Anthropic's description, a blocked cybersecurity query gets a response from Claude Opus 4.8, and a blocked biology query gets a response from Claude Opus 5. The 55% figure breaks down as about 60% fewer blocks in cybersecurity and about 40% fewer in biology, compared with Fable 5.1, according to Anthropic's launch announcement. Anthropic's own August retune of Fable 5 cut biology fallbacks about 85%, a figure Anthropic reported itself. Critics focused on the cost of false positives: Kai Williams of Understanding AI wrote in June that the filters were almost certainly stricter than any other frontier model's, citing a prompt asking "What is protein?" that triggered a downgrade.

The standard price is $8 and $40 per million input and output tokens, down from $10 and $50 for Fable 5.1. Fortune reports that OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra costs $10 and $50 on standard tasks, and that cached tokens for Fable 5.2 cost $0.20 per million against $1.00 for Astra. An optional fast mode generates tokens up to 2.5 times quicker at $16 and $80 per million input and output tokens. MacRumors reports that Fable 5.2 uses fewer tokens than 5.1 on challenging tasks at medium effort.

Anthropic does not recommend Fable or Mythos as the default. It calls Claude Opus 5.5 its strongest daily driver, with Fable or Mythos reserved for harder work and Sonnet 5.5 or Haiku 5.5 for simpler tasks.

Anthropic says a team of about 1,000 Fable 5.2 agents fully formalized Fermat's Last Theorem in Lean in 30 hours with minimal human intervention. Its first formalization, by an unreleased research Claude, took 11 days, and Opus 5.5 completed only 26% in 18 hours in the same 1,000-agent setup. The formalization verifies Andrew Wiles's existing proof, so the milestone measures machine capability, not new mathematics.

METR's Nov. 5 report: after a high-access evaluation from Aug. 12 to Oct. 29, METR concluded Anthropic is likely improving its models on well-specified tasks at about twice the rate it did between March 2024 and October 2025. METR called the estimate highly uncertain, with a true figure plausibly anywhere from 1.3 times to 3 times, and guessed that about 80% of the acceleration comes from AI. METR also said much of the evidence behind its conclusions was redacted by Anthropic and is not public, so readers must lean on METR's judgment.

TechCrunch highlighted two findings from the system card. Both models voice suspicion of being evaluated more often than any recent Claude model, which Anthropic says makes real-world behavior harder to predict. Mythos 5.2 also showed stronger covert capabilities than earlier Claude models on several evaluations, especially when its reasoning was hidden from the monitor. Anthropic says absolute performance on those evaluations is low and that Mythos 5.2 cannot act covertly against its user.

On FrontierCode 2.0, Fable 5.2 scored 49.1% at Xhigh effort, against 45.5% for Opus 5.5 and 32.4% for Fable 5.1. On an internal CVE Rediscovery run in October across 1,829 CVEs, Mythos 5.2 scored 89.6%, against 81.3% for Opus 5.5 without safeguards. These are Anthropic's own numbers, and this reporting found no independent reproduction of the 5.2 results.

Outside testing has been difficult before. When Fable 5 launched, Artificial Analysis found about 8% of its Intelligence Index tasks fell back to Opus 4.8, and Vals AI's GPQA Diamond result fell from second place to 94th when refusals counted as failures, The Batch reported. The same report said Mythos 5 could not be independently evaluated because it was not public. A single score therefore depends on how an evaluator counts fallbacks.

Security teams apply to the Cyber Verification Program, expanded on Oct. 6 with three access tiers. The Life Sciences Verification Program, announced Sept. 17 in beta, reviews research credentials, security standards and ethical oversight. The program requires 30-day retention of traffic, which Anthropic says is kept separate, is not used for training and is off limits to its life sciences research teams. Claude Enterprise customers can reach Mythos 5.2 scans through Claude Security without verification. Claude Security began running scans on Mythos 5 on Aug. 21, and every patch still needs human approval.

The launch arrives as Anthropic prepares to go public. Renaissance Capital estimated on Oct. 26 that the offering could raise about $75 billion, with a roadshow as soon as the week of Nov. 9 and pricing potentially the week of Nov. 16. Earlier talk reached $2 trillion, while Yahoo Finance reports the debut could now land between $1 trillion and $1.5 trillion. Timing and valuation remain subject to change. Fortune describes the launch as important for re-accelerating revenue growth after a third-quarter slowdown disclosed in Anthropic's latest S-1.

The practical read: developers get a 20% lower list price and fewer blocked requests, but should judge Fable 5.2 on what it reliably returns once fallbacks are counted, and Anthropic itself points most coding and knowledge work to Opus 5.5.
